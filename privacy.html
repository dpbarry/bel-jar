BelJar

What BelJar keeps

Without an account, your projects live only in this browser, and BelJar’s server keeps nothing of them. Signing in with GitHub puts them in the cloud, so they are on every device you sign in on. This is everything that keeps, where it is, who can read it, and how to delete it.

What is kept

  • Who you are on GitHub: your account’s number, your handle and name, and the address of your picture, read once each time you sign in. BelJar asks GitHub for no permissions and keeps no GitHub token, so it cannot act for you there. It never asks for your email address.
  • Your projects: every version of each project you have synced: its name, its files and folders, and the text of every file. The cloud keeps history, so a file or project you delete stays in its earlier versions until you delete your account. For BelJar’s limits, it also counts how many projects you have and how much text you have stored.
  • Your settings, while Sync settings is on (Settings, Account).
  • Where you are signed in: for each browser, a rough name for it (“Chrome on Windows”), when you signed in, and when it was last used, to the hour. Signing in puts a random key in a cookie only this site can read; the server keeps only a fingerprint of it, which cannot sign anyone in. A sign-in lasts 90 days. When a browser is signed out from another one, or the account is deleted, the server keeps why under that fingerprint until the 90 days are up, so the browser can do the right thing with its copies when it next visits.

Where

On Cloudflare, which runs BelJar’s server: a database and file storage, both in eastern North America. On your device: your projects and settings, in this browser’s storage for this site, and the sign-in cookie.

Who can read it

You, from any browser you sign in on. Nobody else through BelJar: nothing is shared or published, and there are no ads or analytics. Dean Barry, who runs BelJar, can read the database and storage, and does so only to fix a fault or to answer you. Cloudflare holds them under its own privacy policy. Your GitHub picture is shown from GitHub’s own address.

Logs and backups

Cloudflare logs each request to BelJar’s server for up to 7 days, to find faults: what was asked for, when, how it went, and details of the connection, which can include your network address. The database can be restored to any minute of the last 30 days, and a copy of it is saved each week and kept for four weeks. The text of your files is not in those copies.

Deleting it

  • Everything: Settings, Account, Delete account. Your GitHub details, every browser’s sign-in, your projects with all their versions, and your settings are deleted at once, and the text of your files with them, or within a day if there is a great deal of it. A note that the account is being deleted, holding only its random number, goes within a day too. Every device is signed out, and the projects in your browsers stay there, as each browser’s own. What the logs and backups hold goes as their time runs out, within 30 days.
  • One device: Settings, Account, Devices: Sign out beside it.
  • This browser’s copies: delete a project from home, or clear this site’s data in your browser’s settings.
